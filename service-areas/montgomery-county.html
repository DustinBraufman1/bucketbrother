What’s in the ground here

Southern Montgomery County, including Lower Merion and the Conshohocken area, sits on hard crystalline schist and gneiss. The Whitemarsh and Plymouth valleys are limestone and dolomite, which can weather into voids. Farther north, around Lansdale and Hatfield, red shale is the rule.

Permits and local rules

The Montgomery County Conservation District reviews erosion and sediment control plans and NPDES permits. With 62 municipalities in the county, grading, stormwater and tree rules change from one township line to the next, so we check the local code for every job.

Montgomery County questions

Do you work on the Main Line?

Yes. Ardmore, Bryn Mawr, Narberth and the rest of Lower Merion are in our area, and we plan carefully around mature trees, tight driveways and close neighbors.

Is there limestone under my property?

Parts of Whitemarsh, Plymouth and Upper Merion sit on carbonate rock. If your property is in one of those valleys, it’s worth a closer look at drainage and fill before you build.

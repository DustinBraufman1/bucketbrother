Ground around here changes fast

Greater Philadelphia sits on several kinds of ground. Wissahickon schist runs under northwest Philadelphia and much of Delaware County. Red shale and sandstone underlie upper Bucks and northern Montgomery County. The Chester Valley is limestone, which can hide voids. Older city lots often carry a layer of fill full of brick and old foundations.

That changes the price and the schedule, so we look before we quote. When the ground is a real question, a test pit answers it in a few hours instead of halfway through the job.

Footings, frost and backfill

Footings have to sit below the frost line, which codes in this region generally put between 30 and 36 inches. Your township or the city sets the exact number, and the inspector checks it before concrete goes in.

Backfill matters as much as the dig. We place it in lifts and compact each one so the ground along your foundation doesn’t sink into a trench after the first wet spring.

Where the dirt goes

A basement dig can mean hundreds of cubic yards of soil. Pennsylvania regulates what counts as clean fill and where it can go, so we plan hauling and disposal up front and list it in the estimate as its own line.

What affects the price

  • Depth and size of the dig
  • Access for machines and trucks
  • Rock, old foundations or buried debris
  • How much soil leaves the site
  • Groundwater and pumping
  • Shoring or sloping for deep cuts

Every estimate is free, written and itemized, so you can see where the money goes.

Excavation questions

How deep do footings need to be in the Philadelphia area?

Below the frost line, which codes in this region generally set between 30 and 36 inches. Your municipality sets the exact depth, and the inspector confirms it before the footing is poured.

Can you get a machine into a tight lot or backyard?

Often, yes, with compact equipment. When you ask for an estimate, tell us the narrowest point between the street and the dig and we’ll plan around it.

What happens if you hit rock?

We stop, show you, and talk through the options before we keep going. Rock is the most common reason a dig changes, which is why our estimates spell out how it will be handled.

Do you handle the PA811 call?

Yes. We place the PA One Call request ourselves at least three business days before we dig, as state law requires, and we don’t start until the lines are marked.

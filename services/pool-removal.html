Inground Pool Removal in Greater Philadelphia

A pool nobody swims in still costs money to open, insure and repair. We take it out, backfill it the right way and give you the yard back.

Full or partial removal

There are two ways to remove an inground pool. The right one depends on what you want to do with the space and how long you plan to stay.

Full removalPartial removal
What happensThe whole shell, walls and floor come outThe top of the walls comes off, the floor is broken for drainage and the rest stays buried
CostHigherLower

Selling the houseNothing left to explainBuyers and inspectors will ask about it

The backfill is the job

Most bad pool removals fail in the fill. Dumped dirt settles for years and leaves a sunken patch where the pool was. We place fill in lifts, compact each one, and finish with topsoil so the lawn has something to grow in.

Permits

Most municipalities in the region require a permit to remove a pool, and many inspect the drainage holes or the backfill. Some don’t allow partial removal at all. We check the local rule before we quote.

What affects the price

  • Pool size and type: concrete, gunite, vinyl or fiberglass
  • Full or partial removal
  • Decks, patios and fencing to remove
  • Access to the backyard
  • Fill and topsoil needed

Every estimate is free, written and itemized, so you can see where the money goes.

Pool Removal questions

Should I do a full or partial pool removal?

If you might build, add on or sell soon, full removal is usually worth it. Partial removal costs less and works for lawn or garden, but the old shell stays underground and some towns restrict it.

Can I build where the pool used to be?

After a full removal with properly compacted fill, usually yes. Bring us and your builder together early so the fill is placed and tested to the standard the new structure needs.

Do I need a permit to remove a pool?

In most municipalities around Philadelphia, yes. Requirements and inspections vary, and we’ll confirm them for your address.

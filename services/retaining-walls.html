Retaining Walls in Greater Philadelphia

A retaining wall is mostly the part you can’t see: the base it sits on and the drainage behind it. We build those right so the wall stays straight.

What’s included

  • Segmental block retaining walls
  • Boulder and natural stone walls
  • Excavation and compacted stone base
  • Drain pipe and clean stone behind the wall
  • Geogrid reinforcement where the design calls for it
  • Grading above and below the wall

Walls fail because of water

Soil behind a wall gets heavier when it’s wet, and it pushes harder. Clean stone and a drain pipe behind the wall let that water out before pressure builds. Skip that step and the wall bulges, leans or cracks within a few seasons.

When a wall needs an engineer

Short garden walls are simple. Taller walls, walls holding up a driveway or a building, and walls on steep slopes usually need a permit and an engineered design. Many municipalities draw the line at about four feet. We’ll tell you where yours lands and build from the engineer’s plan when there is one.

What affects the price

  • Height and length of the wall
  • Block or stone selection
  • Excavation and access
  • Drainage and geogrid
  • Engineering and permits for taller walls

Retaining Walls questions

Do I need a permit for a retaining wall?

Short landscape walls often don’t. Taller walls, commonly around four feet and up, and walls that support a driveway, slope or structure usually do, along with an engineered design. Your municipality sets the rule.

Why is my retaining wall leaning?

Usually water pressure behind it, a base that wasn’t compacted, or both. Leaning walls rarely get better on their own, and in many cases the fix is to rebuild with proper drainage.

Should I build a block wall or a boulder wall?

Block walls give clean lines and work well for taller, engineered walls. Boulder walls look natural and suit sloped or wooded lots. Cost depends on height, access and material.
